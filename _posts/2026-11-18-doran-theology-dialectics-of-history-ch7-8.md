Why do people and societies fail to do what they already know to be right? In chapters 7 and 8 of *Theology and the Dialectics of History*, Robert Doran argues that the problem cannot be explained by a lack of knowledge alone. Understanding and moral effort, the creative vector that moves “from below upward,” cannot by themselves reverse personal and historical decline. A gift from above must meet the effort from below.

**Chapter 7, “The Dialectic of the Subject,”** traces how the subject develops through a creative tension between neural demands and the censorship, how four biases (general, individual, group, and dramatic) derail that tension, and why the resulting moral impotence calls for universal willingness given as grace, forming a community in the pattern of the suffering servant.

**Chapter 8, “The Psyche and Integral Interiority,”** turns to the psyche: how spirit and psyche are distinct but not separate, how disordered complexes resist self-transcendence, and how healing love, the healing vector moving “from above downward,” makes psychic conversion possible, so that healing and creativity work together for the sake of authentic historical agency.
