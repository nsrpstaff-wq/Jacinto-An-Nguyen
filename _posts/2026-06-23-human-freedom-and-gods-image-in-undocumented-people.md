title: "Human Freedom and God’s Image in Undocumented People"
category: papers
course: "Published in Obsculta 19, no. 1 (2026): 15–36"
abstract: >-
  This article explores the relationship of human freedom in the theological
  perspective and undocumented immigrants in the United States: how immigrants
  embody the divine image, Imago Dei, despite legal and social constraints. I will
  begin with the theological foundation—freedom as a distinguishing characteristic
  of God’s image in humanity—and analyze the complex nature of freedom as both a
  divine gift and a human responsibility. Additionally, I will demonstrate that the
  Church has a special responsibility to recognize and affirm the inherent dignity
  of undocumented people by drawing from Catholic social teaching, migration
  studies, and theological anthropology, ultimately calling for pastoral practices
  that create spaces of recognition and liberation within existing constraints
  because freedom is a theological concept rather than a political right.
